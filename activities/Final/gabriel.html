Gabriel Medina

3× WSL World Champion — Brazil's First and Greatest Surfing Champion

Biography

Photo: "Gabriel Medina" by Aaron Hughes/World Surf League [Source]

Gabriel Medina, born on December 22, 1993, in Maresias, São Paulo, Brazil, is one of the most technically gifted and athletically explosive surfers in WSL history. With three World Championship titles (2014, 2018, and 2021), Medina became the first Brazilian surfer ever to win the WSL title.

Medina grew up surfing the fast beach breaks near his hometown. He turned professional at 16 and won his first WSL event at just 17.

Known for his mastery of aerial surfing. Medina pushed the boundaries of what was considered possible in competitive surfing and opened the air-focused scoring era on the tour. [WSL — Gabriel Medina]

Career Highlights

Making History — 2014 World Title

Gabriel Medina won his first WSL World Title in 2014 at just 20 years old, becoming the first Brazilian ever to claim the championship. He won the title at the Billabong Pipe Masters in Hawaii.

Second Title — 2018

Medina won his second world title in 2018. The WSL tour in 2018 was arguably the deepest field in history, with talents like Filipe Toledo, and Italo Ferreira. Super hard to win.

Third Title — 2021

Medina's third WSL title in 2021 confirmed that he is one of the sport's all-time greats. Winning three championships in the modern, ultra-competitive era requires a level of mental toughness and physical skill that very few athletes have. [Wikipedia — Gabriel Medina]

The Art of Aerial Surfing

One of Gabriel Medina's most significant contributions to professional surfing is his role in popularizing and elevating aerial surfing.

His signature move, the Reverse 360, involves spinning the board a full rotation in the air above the wave. In competition, a well-executed aerial can earn scores approaching a perfect 10, and Medina has built a reputation as the most reliable aerial surfer in the world.

His influence has reshaped how judges score surfing and what the next generation of surfers aspires to achieve in the water. [Wikipedia — Gabriel Medina]
