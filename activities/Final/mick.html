Mick Fanning

3× WSL World Champion — "White Lightning" from Australia

Biography

Photo: "Mick Fanning" via CNN [Source]

Michael Eugene Fanning, born on June 13, 1981, in Penrith, New South Wales, Australia, is one of the most Australian surfers in WSL history. Known by his nickname "White Lightning," Fanning earned three WSL World Championship titles and is universally respected for his powerful backhand surfing, and unwavering competitive focus.

Throughout his career, Fanning won 22 Championship Tour, placing him among the most successful contest surfers of all time. He retired from full-time WSL competition in 2018. [Wikipedia — Mick Fanning]

Career Highlights

First World Title — 2007

Mick Fanning's first World Title in 2007 was a statement to the surfing world that Australia had a new powerhouse competitor. He surfed with precision and power throughout the season, winning multiple events and building a lead in the rankings that he never relinquished. His backhand attack — the ability to surf with his back facing the wave — was considered one of the best in the world. [Wikipedia — Fanning World Titles]

Second Title — 2009

Two years after his first championship, Fanning returned to claim his second title in 2009. His ability to perform under pressure in critical heats — especially in the finals of events — was one of the defining features of his competitive record. He was well-known for raising his performance in the moments that mattered most.

Third Title — 2013

Fanning's 2013 World Title was arguably his most impressive, coming after years of strong results but no championship. He surfed a near-flawless season and clinched the title at the Pipeline Masters in Hawaii, completing a remarkable year that reaffirmed his status as one of the elite surfers of his era. The 2013 title was particularly emotional as it came just years after the death of his brother Sean, who had been a constant source of motivation for Mick.

The J-Bay Shark Attack — 2015

On July 19, 2015, during the finals of the J-Bay Open at Jeffreys Bay, South Africa. While waiting for a wave in the final against Julian Wilson, Mick Fanning was attacked by a large Great White Shark.

Fanning fought off the shark with his fist, punching it before it dragged him underwater by his leg rope. The video of the incident went viral within hours and was watched by millions worldwide.

In interviews after the incident, Fanning spoke about the fear, the adrenaline, and the reflection he had about what truly matters in life. He took time off from competition to process the experience before returning to the tour. [Wikipedia — Mick Fanning Shark Attack]
